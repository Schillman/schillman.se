Helltides and the cinder economy

Helltide is the most misplayed activity in the game, and it is misplayed in exactly one way: people bank cinders they never get to spend. Season 14 also quietly turned Helltide into the venue for the season mechanic, which makes an hour spent here worth more than it looks.

The currency

Aberrant Cinders, and the rule that costs people loot

Everything inside a Helltide runs on Aberrant Cinders. They come off monsters as you kill things, and the rate is more predictable than it feels in the moment:

  • Roughly one in every five monster kills grants cinders, on average.
  • Elites are guaranteed to grant at least one.
  • Treasure Goblins grant 1 to 6.

Now the important part, and the reason this page exists. Cinders do not carry over between events. A Helltide runs for 55 minutes, and when it ends, every cinder you were saving is gone. Not banked, not converted, gone.

That single rule should restructure how you play the hour. Cinders are not savings, they are a perishable resource on a timer, and the correct behaviour is to spend down to near zero before the clock runs out rather than to hold a balance for a chest you have not found yet. If you die you also drop a portion of what you were carrying, which is the second argument for spending as you go rather than at the end.

The practical version Open chests continuously through the hour, and start actively hunting a chest to dump into at around the ten minute mark. A 250 cinder Mystery chest opened with two minutes left is worth infinitely more than 240 cinders that expire tidily in your pocket.

Spending

What every Tortured Gift costs

Tortured Gifts are the chests scattered around the zone, and they are priced by what they contain. All of them roll for Legendary and Unique items and pay materials on the side, including Forgotten Souls and Baneful Hearts. Prices as listed by Maxroll's Helltide guide:

Tortured Gift costs in Aberrant Cinders, and what each one is for.
ChestCostWhen it is the right chest
Armour, jewellery, weapons 75 to 150 The bread and butter. Priced by slot, so the cheap end is the efficient one if you are only after volume and materials rather than a specific piece.
Amulets 125 Worth targeting deliberately. Amulets carry more weight per item than most slots, so a fixed 125 for a shot at one is a better rate than it appears next to the 75 chests.
Two handed weapons 150 Only if your build actually uses one. Otherwise this is the most expensive way to buy salvage.
Tortured Gift of Mysteries 250 The high tier chest, and the one to dump a large balance into late in the hour. Also the target if you are farming Forgotten Souls specifically.
Hell's Prize 666 A War Plan reward rather than a chest you find lying around. The largest single sink available, which makes it the answer to the end of hour problem if you have one active.

Pressure

The Threat meter and Kixxarth

Killing things inside a Helltide fills a Threat meter. As it fills you get ambushed, and at the top it summons a Hellborne boss onto you. This is a reward mechanism dressed as a punishment: the ambushes and the boss are additional elite density in a zone where elites are guaranteed cinders.

Letting Threat build is therefore correct unless your build genuinely cannot survive it. The failure case is dying with a large cinder balance, which loses you a portion of it, so the safe pattern is to let Threat climb while your pockets are light and spend down before you deliberately cap it.

Separately, Kixxarth, the Helltide Assassin roams the zone. It hits considerably harder than the ambient content and pays cinders and Legendaries for the kill. Treat it as an event worth crossing the map for rather than something to avoid.

Season 14

Why an hour in Helltide is worth more this season

In most seasons Helltide is one activity competing with the others for your evening. In Season of Death Awakening it is the venue for the season mechanic, and that changes the arithmetic:

  • Normal Ruptures are dense inside a Helltide, and far rarer outside one.
  • Surging Ruptures only exist inside a Helltide, where they replace local events, and finishing one with Mastery is your chance at a Realmwalker.
  • So the cinders, the Ruptures and the Glints of Hope all accrue from the same monsters, in the same hour, without you choosing between them.

The full chain from a Rupture to a Superior Lair Key to a Mythic Unique is on the Season of Death Awakening page. What matters here is simply that you should be doing your Helltide inside the season loop rather than as a separate errand.

War Plans

The two nodes that change this activity most

The Helltide tree in War Plans holds the strongest single combination in the game right now, and both nodes act on Hellwyrms, the surfacing worms you will already be killing:

  • Hellmouth makes Hellwyrms produce something extra every time they surface.
  • Writhe and Rot forces that output to be only Maggots, instead of splitting between Chaos Rifts, Bloodseekers and Maggots. Maggots drop Experience Orbs, which is what makes this the fastest levelling and Paragon combination available.

Take them in that order, and take them before anything else in any tree. Levelling faster raises the Torment tier you can hold, and Activity Experience for every other War Plan tree scales with difficulty, so this pair makes the rest of the system cheaper to fill.

Spelling matters here The node is Writhe and Rot, not "Wither and Rot". The wrong version is copied between community guides constantly, including in this site's own Ledger for the first week of the season. If you are searching and finding nothing, that is usually why.

In one paragraph

The loop

Take Hellmouth and Writhe and Rot first. Sit in an active Helltide for the full 55 minutes rather than dipping in. Kill elites on the way past everything, let Threat build while your balance is low, chase Kixxarth and any Goblin you see, break Death's Head Idols as they appear, and open chests continuously so you are never holding more cinders than you can afford to lose. From ten minutes out, spend everything, on a Mystery chest if you can find one and on whatever is nearest if you cannot.

That is five habits, and four of them are the kind of thing that quietly stops happening by week three. The Season 14 Farming Ledger is where I keep the tickable version.
